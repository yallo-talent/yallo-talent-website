## Platform

## Users

**Primary — enterprise buyers of platform programme talent.** They are mid-programme,
under a gate date, and short of specific people. Seven buying roles recur:

| Role | The situation they are in |
|---|---|
| Delivery Director | Won the work faster than they can staff it |
| Head of Talent Acquisition | Buried in CV volume, starved of fit |
| PMO Director | Every open seat shows red on the status pack |
| VP Engineering | Roadmap is fine; the bench is not |
| Practice Lead | Certified is not the same as delivered |
| CIO | Owns the programme outcome |
| Procurement | Needs published terms and a defensible rate basis |

They arrive knowing what SAP or Oracle *is*. They do not need the platform explained.
They need to know whether Yallo can find six specific people, by when, and on what terms.

**Not a primary audience: candidates.** Client-first IA is ratified. Candidates are served
by the job board via one quiet punchout to `/jobs`. No candidate-facing content sits in the
buyer path.

## Product Purpose

Yallo Talent staffs and delivers enterprise platform programmes across the Middle East and
Europe with specialist-screened people, including AI talent.

Success on this site is a **programme brief**, not a vacancy — a buyer describing the phase
they are in and the gate they are working to. Secondary conversion is a request for the
gated Programme Staffing Blueprint.

## Positioning

**The wedge is enterprise platform depth at module level.** The closest competitor (Halian —
same four-pillar shape, same three regions) organises by profession and treats ERP as a
single line item. No firm in the benchmarked set publishes SAP, Oracle and Microsoft with
module-level role coverage across the Middle East and Europe. Yallo already holds the
taxonomy and the client list to stand there.

Three supporting positions, each verified unclaimed in the category:

- **A commitment on the shortlist, not just the placement.** Competitors guarantee the hire;
  nobody publishes terms on the shortlist itself.
- **The programme staffing view instead of the salary guide.** Four of six competitors publish
  a salary guide. None publishes what it takes to staff a programme role by role and phase by
  phase — it requires both placement data and programme knowledge.
- **AI talent as a named specialism with an owner**, not a skill listed among professions.

**Boundary with the sibling brand.** Yallo Talent delivers fixed scope on the client's
*existing* enterprise platforms. saasinator builds *new* AI-native systems the client owns.
Routing test: "make our Salesforce work" is Talent; "replace the thing we rent" is saasinator.

## Operating Context

**Four pillars, in this order of commercial weight:**

1. **Contract** — direct to end client. The lead motion.
2. **Permanent** — account entry and credibility.
3. **Employer of Record** — an enabler *inside* contract, not a standalone product.
4. **Managed Delivery** — fixed-scope work on the client's existing platforms.

**Markets, in priority order:** Middle East (UAE, Saudi Arabia) primary · Europe (UK first) a
genuine second demand market · India third, de-prioritised but real, specifically **Global
Capability Centre staffing for multinationals** — a demand market, never a delivery or supply
function.

**Four legal entities:** London · Dubai · Riyadh · Bengaluru. Three demand markets, four
entities. There is no "three delivery regions" claim.

**The mechanism buyers are actually evaluating** is the screen: brief → calibrate → screen →
shortlist, inside 72 hours, with rejection reasons attached. Six specialist desks sit behind
it (Architecture, Software Development, Cloud & Infrastructure, Packaged Software,
Data & Analytics, Agile & DevOps) with one account manager as single point of contact.

## Capabilities and Constraints

**Taxonomy is fixed.** Six platforms in this order, because Yallo is a Microsoft house:
SAP · Oracle · Microsoft (Azure and Dynamics 365, at equal depth to Oracle) · Salesforce ·
Blue Yonder · Workday. ServiceNow and AWS are not in the platform set; AWS folds into
cloud-infrastructure.

Six sectors: retail · finance · manufacturing · government · healthcare · telco.

Six disciplines: `data-analytics` · `cloud-infrastructure` · `cybersecurity` ·
`integration-middleware` · `devops-platform-engineering` · `testing-quality-engineering`.

Eight role families: Architects · Application Consultants · Developers · Data specialists ·
Cloud and platform · Test and quality · Business and functional · Product and agile.

Segments are an in-page concept only, never a page family.

**Terminology is contractual, not stylistic.**

- "Middle East" is canonical. **"GCC" is banned outright** — in Yallo's own India business it
  means Global Capability Centre, colliding with the exact data the site cites.
- Name UAE and Saudi Arabia explicitly. Never "KSA" in public copy.
- **Bengaluru**, never Bangalore.
- Pillar four is **Managed Delivery**. "Subcontract" and "subcontracting" never appear in
  public copy.
- "AI talent" is the category name. "Claude talent" is depth-proof only.
- UK English throughout.

**Commercial constraints.**

- **No rates or fee percentages on public pages.** Every competitor converts to consultation
  with no published price. Rate bands live only inside the gated Blueprint.
- **Database size is deliberately not published** — it argues against the screening positioning
  and invites a bench-depth comparison against the Indian SIs.
- Published commitments are limited to the real contractual terms below. The earlier "first ten
  working days at our risk" line is **withdrawn**: it creates unbounded exposure where
  contractors are on Yallo visa sponsorship.

**Content rules.** Stop describing tools, deploy talent. Never write vendor marketing voice,
superlatives without evidence, or unsourced figures. Write what roles Yallo places, how
screening works, and why the contractor has implementation depth.

**Technical constraints.** Next.js 16 App Router · TypeScript strict · Tailwind 4 · pnpm 10 ·
Node 22 · Framer Motion 12. Static-generated, no CMS at launch. Raw hex may exist in exactly
one file, enforced by a pre-commit hook. Destination domain is yallo.co; any non-production
host must be `noindex`.

## Product Principles

1. **Answer "can you find my six people, by when, on what terms" above the fold.** Every
   section either proves capability, proves speed, or states terms. Anything that does none of
   those is decoration.
2. **Specificity is the moat.** The category's copy is interchangeable. A sentence a competitor
   could also write has failed, however well it is set.
3. **Depth beats breadth, at module level.** Platform depth is the wedge; publishing it is the
   differentiator. Never flatten enterprise platforms into a profession list.
4. **Publish only what survives a screenshot.** Every number carries a definition, every client
   carries consent, every commitment is a real contractual term. A competitor screenshotting any
   surface must find nothing to attack.
5. **The buyer's path is never shared with the candidate's.** One quiet punchout, and no
   candidate content in between.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA is a self-imposed gate on **every surface, in both themes independently** — not a
best-effort target. Alongside it: Lighthouse Mobile 90+, LCP under 2.5s, CLS under 0.1, INP
under 200ms, and responsive behaviour down to 360px.

Specific commitments:

- **Meaning never depends on hue alone.** Every functional colour carries an equivalent label or
  icon. This is load-bearing: the site encodes scarcity and availability, which are exactly the
  signals a colour-blind buyer would otherwise lose.
- All motion is gated on `prefers-reduced-motion`, including count-up animations — the current
  build animates regardless, which is a live breach.
- Real values are server-rendered. Animating *from* zero is permitted; rendering zero to a
  crawler or a non-scrolling reader is not.
- Interactive coverage grids and persona panels are keyboard operable with visible focus.
- UK English, `lang="en-GB"`.
