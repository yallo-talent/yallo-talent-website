# Yallo Talent — Phase 1 Benchmark

**Version 1.0 · 29 July 2026 · Project GTM.01 — Websites — Product Build**
Analyst: Claude (Chat lens), directed by Sumeet Goenka
Purpose: establish what converts in this category before designing the homepage, and identify the ground nobody has taken.

**Method and limits.** Read: published homepages, service pages, market landing pages and profession pages across the firms below, plus Robert Half's disclosed commercial terms. Not done: paid-media teardown, session recordings, or any access to their analytics. Scores are my judgement from published surfaces on a 1 to 5 scale, not an audited measurement. Where I could not verify something I have left it blank rather than guessed.

---

## 1. The rubric

Ten criteria, chosen because each one maps to a decision the homepage has to make.

| # | Criterion | What a 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Buyer-facing IA | Every nav label answers a buyer question, not an org chart |
| 2 | Client versus candidate separation | Buyers never navigate around candidate content |
| 3 | Proof model | Named clients, sourced numbers, attributed testimonials |
| 4 | Consultant presentation | Named specialists with domain and direct contact |
| 5 | Lead magnet and gating | A distinctive asset worth an email, in primary nav |
| 6 | SEO surface and cadence | Deep, current, taxonomy-aligned content |
| 7 | Conversion mechanics | Multiple contextual asks, low friction, clear next step |
| 8 | Risk reversal | An explicit commitment that reduces buyer risk |
| 9 | Copy specificity | Concrete claims a competitor could not also make |
| 10 | Visual distinctiveness | Recognisable without the logo |

## 3. What the category actually does

**Halian is the closest competitor and its own material shows where it stops.** Positioned as a managed services and IT recruitment firm across Europe, the Middle East and the USA, founded in London in 1996, with a Saudi office opened in 2023. Services run permanent, contract, managed services, outsourced payroll, RPO, executive search and Employer of Record. Their taxonomy is organised by **profession**: cyber security, infrastructure and cloud, data and AI, projects and change, software engineering, finance, digital. ERP appears as a single line item.

That last detail is the most important finding in this document. **Halian has no per-platform depth.** There is no SAP page with module-level coverage, no Oracle page, no Workday page. The firm that is otherwise building exactly Yallo's business treats enterprise platform work as one entry in a profession list.

**Halian's strongest transferable pattern is market-specific landing pages.** They run localised variants at country level, each with its own headline written for that market rather than a translated global page. Yallo needs the same for the Middle East, Europe and Saudi Arabia specifically.

**Michael Page leads on conversion mechanics and proof volume.** Their homepage opens with scalable hiring support, carries a volume claim about the number of companies that hired through them in the past year, and runs attributed client testimonials using a first name, a surname initial and the company. Three clean service tiers: recruitment services, executive search, enterprise solutions.

**Robert Half is the only firm in the set with real risk reversal.** Their public commercial position is consultation-led with no published rate card, permanent fees calculated as a percentage of annual compensation, and their own filings reference a ninety-day guarantee period. Nobody else in this field offers a commitment of that kind.

**Forsyth Barnes wins one criterion outright.** Named consultants with individual specialisms and direct contact routes, and salary guides sitting in primary navigation as a first-class destination. They are weak on sector overlap with Yallo, so they are a presentation model rather than a threat.

**Nobody publishes rates.** Every firm in the set converts to a consultation or a callback. This validates keeping rate bands inside the gated Blueprint and off the public site.

**Everybody's copy is interchangeable.** Across all six: top-notch talent, trusted partner, unlock your potential, seamless process, tailored solutions. Not one sentence on any of these homepages could not appear on any other. This is the widest open flank in the category.

## 4. Steal

Seven patterns that work and that Yallo does not currently have.

| Pattern | Source | Application |
|---|---|---|
| **Market-specific landing pages** with market-written headlines, not translated boilerplate | Halian | `/middle-east`, `/europe`, and `/saudi-arabia` given its own page because of the in-country entity |
| **A volume proof claim in the first screen**, specific and dated | Michael Page | Placements or programmes staffed, with an "as at" date and a definition |
| **Attributed testimonials**, name plus initial plus company | Michael Page | Cheapest credibility upgrade available. Yallo has zero |
| **Risk reversal as a published commitment** | Robert Half | See section 6. This is the biggest single opportunity |
| **Lead magnet in primary navigation**, not buried in a resources page | Forsyth Barnes | The Programme Staffing Blueprint under Intelligence |
| **Named consultants with specialism and direct contact** | Forsyth Barnes | The team who screen, inside Evidence |
| **FAQ blocks with schema** on service and platform pages | Halian | Cheap SEO surface and it answers procurement questions |

## 5. Avoid

| Anti-pattern | Seen in | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Profession-only taxonomy with ERP as one line | Halian | Abandons the platform depth that is Yallo's wedge |
| Undifferentiated superlative copy | All six | Interchangeable claims persuade nobody and Yallo already beats this |
| Candidate content in the buyer's path | Michael Page, Hays | Client-first is ratified; the board serves candidates |
| Years-in-business as the lead credential | Halian, Robert Half | Yallo cannot win a longevity contest and should not enter one |
| Stock photography carrying the emotional load | All six | Identical image libraries across the whole category |
| Service tiles as the organising idea | Halian, Michael Page | Fragments the pitch and invites line-by-line commoditisation |

## 6. Own

Five positions that are unclaimed in this field. Each is defensible because it requires something the incumbents do not have.

**1. Enterprise platform depth at module level.** Halian treats ERP as a line item. Whitehall covers SAP only. Nobody publishes SAP, Oracle and Microsoft with module-level role coverage across Middle East and Europe. Yallo's existing taxonomy and logo wall already sit here. This is the wedge.

**2. Named, architect-led screening with faces.** The category standard is a named team page, and only Forsyth Barnes clears it. Everyone else presents an anonymous firm. Yallo makes architect-led screening its central claim and currently shows no humans, which is the worst of both positions.

**3. Risk reversal on the shortlist.** Robert Half guarantees the placement. Nobody guarantees the shortlist. A published commitment such as three screened candidates within 72 hours or the search is free would be unique in the Middle East market, costs nothing when the screening actually works, and converts precisely the buyer who has been burned by CV volume. **[REC]** Ratify a specific commitment in canon before launch. This is the single highest-leverage addition on this page.

**4. The programme staffing view rather than the salary guide.** Four of six publish a salary guide. None publishes what it takes to staff a programme role by role and phase by phase. Nobody can, without both placement data and programme knowledge.

**5. AI talent as a named specialism.** Confirmed again here: Halian lists AI and machine learning as a managed-services technology and as a profession, not as a practice with an owner and a body of published work. The category treats AI as a skill to source, not a category to lead.

## 7. The homepage funnel this implies

WHY, WHAT, HOW, PROOF, CTA, adapted for a contract-led enterprise motion. Twelve sections, light register with two dark evidence bands, three contextual asks rather than one at the bottom.

| # | Section | Funnel stage | Job on the page | Band |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hero** | WHY | One claim, one primary ask, four-entity footprint. Single frame, no carousel | Light |
| 2 | **The cost of the gap** | WHY | The buyer's problem in their own terms, one sourced figure | Light |
| 3 | **Proof** | PROOF | Metrics with definitions and an "as at" date, then two logo walls: accounts owned, and the integrators who buy Yallo's specialists. **Ask 1** | Light |
| 4 | **The screen** | HOW | The four-phase brief-to-shortlist mechanism as the structural signature. The differentiator, high on the page | Light |
| 5 | **The people who screen** | PROOF | Named architects, specialism, platforms, region, direct contact. New section | Light |
| 6 | **The commitment** | HOW | The risk-reversal guarantee, stated plainly. New section, and unique in the category | Light |
| 7 | **How you engage** | WHAT | One proposition, four models beneath: contract leading, then permanent, EOR, Managed Delivery | Light |
| 8 | **Platform depth** | WHAT | SAP, Oracle, Microsoft first, then Salesforce, ServiceNow, Blue Yonder, Workday. The wedge made visible | **Dark** |
| 9 | **AI talent** | WHAT | The category claim, routing to `/ai-talent`. New section | **Dark** |
| 10 | **Case studies** | PROOF | Real names, real numbers, attributed testimonials. **Ask 2** | Light |
| 11 | **Intelligence** | PROOF | The Blueprint gated, the Atlas open, insights beneath | Light |
| 12 | **Start a brief** | CTA | The close. Programme-shaped, not vacancy-shaped. **Ask 3** | Light |

Sections 8 and 9 take the dark band because both are evidence and data surfaces, which is the rule ratified earlier. `The Yallo Family` group carousel is removed and becomes one reason-to-believe line inside section 5, where it supports the screening claim.

**Three sections do not exist yet and are the reason the current homepage does not convert:** the people who screen, the commitment, and AI talent. Two of the three need no new data.

---

## 8. What Yallo must decide before the homepage is built

1. **The risk-reversal commitment.** Exact wording and exact terms. Section 6 cannot be designed around a placeholder. **[REC]** three architect-screened candidates within 72 hours of a complete brief, or the search carries no fee.
2. **The headline metric and its definition.** Section 3 needs one number that means exactly one thing, with a date and an owner.
3. **Two named architects minimum** for section 5, with real names and specialisms. Photographs can follow.
4. **One attributed testimonial** for section 10. Name, initial, company, and permission.

Items 1 and 2 are yours alone. Items 3 and 4 need a client or colleague to say yes.
