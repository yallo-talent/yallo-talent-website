## 1. Shipped

### A — the editorial cockpit, to the Substack bar

**A1, the lifecycle rail.** The complaint that opened this round was that the
draft → update → publish → unpublish workflow could not be worked out from the
surface. Every editor now carries a persistent sticky rail with three things and
nothing else: a **status pill whose word is the state**, a **live Saved pill**
reduced from all three writers on the canvas, and **exactly one primary action**
chosen by that state — Draft gets *Continue* into the publish sheet, Published
gets *Update*, Archived gets *Return to draft*. Unpublish, Archive, View live
and the URL sit one click away under *More*, and the actions that are not legal
in a state are not drawn at all. Preview is one click and always present. A
first-run explainer states the cycle in two sentences on any piece that has
never been live, and on each pane's empty state.

**A2 and R-26.5, the canvas.** Title and Subtitle are placeholder fields at the
top of the canvas, set in the faces they publish in. **The subtitle is the
summary column** — one value, one store, per R-26.5 — so the drawer no longer
carries either field, and both autosave through a third writer with its own
disjoint columns. The byline is shown as the system applies it, with nothing to
type a name into. A **persistent top toolbar** joins the floating selection
toolbar and the slash menu: the first is the one a person finds, the other two
are what they graduate to. The metadata scroll became a **drawer**, grouped into
Filing, Search and sharing, Address and History, server-rendered and passed
through the client boundary so the taxonomy indexes stay out of the browser
bundle.

**A2A, the block seam.** One new embed node, **YouTube**, stored as the
eleven-character video id and never a URL, so the renderer composes an address
it can trust rather than validating one it was handed. Privacy-enhanced host,
lazy-loaded, inside a 16:9 box so a third-party document cannot move the text
under a reader's eyes. The registry the toolbar and the slash menu both read is
**one file** now; before this round the slash menu held the list and the toolbar
did not exist. `spec/block-registry.md` records the seam: adding audio, native
video or a carousel is four declarations and **no migration of any stored body**.

**A3, the register.** Chips with a real checkbox inside replace raw checkboxes;
one control height throughout the drawer; labels moved to Inter with IBM Plex
Mono retreating to identifiers, counters and timestamps; the full-width gold
slab became a compact save at the foot of the fields it saves; History became a
timeline rather than stacked cards.

**A4, impeccable across the panes.** `check:admin-render` now visits **seven**
panes plus three detail templates, across two themes and two widths — 44
contexts. `/admin/media` was missing from its list and is on it now; the gate
passes with **zero** blocking findings.

**A5, media in the flow.** An insert dialog carrying the library, an upload and
the alt text in one act, so alt text stays mandatory in practice rather than in
principle and a freshly uploaded image arrives with its rendition set. The
upload route now answers in the same shape as the list, which it did not — a
freshly uploaded image would have been placed with no `srcset` at all, spending
the resizing pipeline backwards, and nothing would have looked wrong.

### C — R-26.1, validation never blocks

`BLOCKING_RULES` is an **empty set**, and the spec asserts it is empty so a
future round has to change that line deliberately against a red test. **Not one
detection assertion changed**: every rule finds what it found, with the same
message, naming the same text. What changed is what a caller may do with a
finding, which is show it. The refusal assertions became warning-surface ones;
the messages that promised a refusal no longer happening were reworded.

The **publish sheet** lists whatever is outstanding and publishes on a single
confirm, and it is explicit on the page that none of it stops the publish.

The **safety net** is a re-validation of what is already live, computed on every
pane render rather than stored — a stored finding is one that can be stale, and
the failure this guards against is somebody trusting a clean panel computed
before the piece was edited. It surfaces as a notices strip above the list on
Articles and Case studies, silent when there is nothing.

The **unpublish guard** is unchanged and still refuses: it is link integrity,
not editorial judgement. Its message is now an explanation naming the addresses
and the single action that resolves it, rather than a diagnosis in the
vocabulary of the redirect table.

### B — the funnel

Briefs was every capture printed as a pretty-printed payload. It is now a lead
surface: five pipeline states with one click between them and a count per state;
an owner assignable from the live account list; an **SLA clock against the
72-hour commitment the site publishes**, banded rather than ramped, stopped at
Contacted because the promise is about a reply reaching somebody; **lead cards**
with the raw payload behind a disclosure; filters, search, saved views and CSV
export all in the URL; conversation linkage both ways; and an origin rollup that
says its data starts on 8 August 2026 rather than inferring backwards.

`submissions` **stays append-only**. Every write goes to `submission_funnel`, a
sidecar keyed one-to-one; an absent sidecar row means New, so no second write
sits in the path of the capture the backstop exists to protect. Every change
writes a `content_audit` row. Nothing deletes: Lost is a state.

The export **imports the pane's own filter function** rather than
reimplementing it. CSV cells are quoted unconditionally and anything a
spreadsheet would execute is prefixed, because this is text a stranger typed
into a public form.

The delivery email now names the cockpit URL of the new lead, on all three
capture routes. It did not.

## 3. Retractions

**One, and it is from this document.** An earlier draft of §8 recorded
`check:signature-gif` as green and "byte-identical to a rebuild". That was true
of the run I watched and **false as a general claim**, and the gate's design was
wrong with it.

CI proved it on Linux: same length, same dimensions, same frame count, first
difference at **byte 16** — inside the global colour table. libvips quantises a
palette slightly differently between platform builds, so two encoders emit the
same picture through a different table. The gate as first written would have
red-blocked every pull request raised from a machine unlike the one that last
built the binary, which is a gate reporting the toolchain rather than the asset.

Corrected in `53a008b`: it decodes both files and compares the **frames as
pixels**, which is what the gate cares about — what a recipient sees. Controlled
with a real change rather than a flipped byte: `"shortlisted in 72 hours"` to
`"48 hours"` in the load-bearing first frame gives exit 1, 0.708% of channels
differing, a worst delta of 220 against a limit of 8, located in frame 0.
Restored: exit 0, 0.000% differing.

**The lesson is the retraction's real content.** A gate proven on one machine is
proven on one machine. "Deterministic over the same inputs" needed the words
"and the same toolchain", and the local run could not have told me that. Nothing
in v36 or earlier is withdrawn.

## 4. Delegated decisions, with their reversals

1. **The unpublish guard's message was rewritten, and its spec assertion with
   it.** The old assertion required the word "two-hop"; the new message does not
   use it, because it is not a phrase the person clicking Unpublish has any
   reason to know. The spec now asserts the properties R-26.1 asks for instead:
   the study's own address, what would happen, and the one action.
   *Reversal:* restore the old string and the old assertion together.

2. **Rule 7 covers embeds as well as images.** A video with no caption and an
   image with no alt text are one fault — media offering nothing to a reader who
   cannot or will not consume it — so it is the same rule number rather than a
   tenth. The consequence is that the next embed type is covered by a rule that
   already exists rather than by one somebody must remember to write.
   *Reversal:* split it into rule 10 and give it its own field.

3. **`briefsWrite` was honoured, not widened.** See open item 1.

4. **No new runtime dependency.** Nothing was added to `dependencies`. The brand
   pipeline's `@resvg/resvg-js` and the pinned fonts are local to
   `scripts/brand/`, already gitignored there, and do not ship.

## 5. Open items, each with the question that unblocks it

1. **Can ops move a lead through the pipeline?** Round 25 named `briefsWrite` as
   owner and admin, with the reason recorded: so the first write path would
   arrive against an admin-only rule rather than inherit the read rule by
   accident. Round 26 built that write path and **honoured the entry**. The
   consequence is real: ops — the role whose stated job is following leads up —
   can read and export every lead and cannot move one, so in practice an
   administrator has to move every lead for them. It is asserted in
   `e2e/roles.spec.ts` so changing it is deliberate.
   **Question: should `ops` join `briefsWrite`?** It is one line in
   `CAPABILITY_ROLES` plus the matrix in the spec.

2. **The public site chrome renders around the cockpit.** Every admin pane draws
   the public navigation bar, the theme toggle and the "Ask Yallo Talent"
   launcher above and over it, because the cockpit sits under the root layout.
   It is pre-existing, out of this round's scope, and it costs a real strip of
   vertical space plus a floating control that overlaps the funnel's cards at
   1280. **Question: should round 27 give `/admin` its own root layout?**

3. **Does the safety net need to reach somebody who has not opened the pane?**
   The strip is live on every render, which is the strong half. The weak half is
   that a warned figure sitting on a published page is only seen by whoever next
   opens Articles. **Question: is a scheduled job that emails the findings worth
   building, or is the strip enough given Raphy's team is in the cockpit daily?**

## 6a. One design finding, left unchanged, with the reasoning

The impeccable hook reports `side-tab` on `.pullQuote` in
`src/components/blocks/editorial/YalloBlocks.module.css:25` — a 3px accent rule
down the left edge.

**It is pre-existing** (round 25b, `f8dc9bf`) and this round did not touch it;
the hook re-scanned the file because round 26 added the `.embed` rules to it.

**It is left as it is, and not suppressed.** The rule targets "a thick coloured
border on one side of a *card*", which is a real tell. `.pullQuote` is not a
card: it is a `<figure>` wrapping a `<blockquote>`, and a rule down the left
edge of a quotation is the oldest convention in typesetting. The estate is
already consistent about the distinction — `.block` in `Editor.module.css`
carries a comment explicitly refusing a side tab *on a card*, and
`.prose blockquote` uses the same left rule this does.

**No ignore entry was added**, because suppressing a finding is a decision for
Sumeet rather than for a session, and nothing about this one is urgent. If he
agrees it is a false positive, the narrowest form is:

```
/impeccable hooks ignore-value side-tab "*" --file "src/components/blocks/editorial/YalloBlocks.module.css"
```
